# I-0001, Moving-Frame Velocity Prevented Deployment

> **Navigation**: [Incidents](README.md) | [Verification evidence](../verification/VR-0010.md)

**Date and author.** 2026-11-17, Codex.
**Classification.** Design and control fault under R10, not a claimed game defect.
**Status.** Corrected in development. Broader flight qualification remains open.

## Timeline and consequence

M-0001 hop 1 ignited an empty IKR-TS-001B. Its 1,982 recorded samples span 39.62 game
seconds and reach 1,238.78 metres above terrain. The Keleusma program never requested
a parachute because the bridge reported zero vertical speed. The bridge subsequently
reported a destroyed part and disconnected. A later inspection found one surviving
part. No kerbal was aboard, and the career save was not reloaded or changed.

## Cause and contributing factors

The bridge constructed flight telemetry in a frame moving with the vessel. Altitude
was meaningful in that frame, while velocity was zero. The recovery probe also used
a default moving frame. It reached the descending article at about 979 metres but
reported zero vertical speed, so its deployment condition never became true. The
intervention therefore failed for the same class of error, rather than merely arriving
late. No physical loss was attributed to a game defect.

The scripted fixtures supplied velocity directly and could not detect the incorrect
game reference frame. Pad monitoring also could not distinguish a valid zero velocity
from this defect. The initial integration lacked a moving-state frame check before
allowing the flight program to depend on descent rate.

## Corrective action and acceptance

Velocity is now measured in the rotating body frame. Angular velocity is measured in
that frame and transformed to vessel coordinates. The moving surface frame supplies
only the local up direction. Subsequent runs showed positive ascent, negative descent,
Keleusma deployment commands and intact recoveries. The unmodified career hash was
checked again after the series. No campaign completion criterion is credited.

The failed journal, bridge error and intervention observations remain in VR-0010's
data. Future articles still need independent frame and actuation validation while
moving. Software replay alone cannot establish the correctness of game telemetry.

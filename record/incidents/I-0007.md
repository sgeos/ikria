# I-0007, Jebediah Kerman Marked Missing in the Verification Save

> **Navigation**: [Incidents](README.md) | [Verification evidence](../verification/VR-0011.md)

**Date and author.** 2026-11-18, a Claude line.
**Classification.** Crew-safety fault under R10 and R17. Kerbal loss, reported under R11.
**Status.** Open. The operator decides whether to restore the kerbal in the verification save.

## Timeline and consequence

Before each test the game seated a kerbal in the Mk1 pod, and the script sent the
kerbal on EVA and recovered them before any firing. In the first soft-landing run the
EVA failed, because a sepratron covered the pod's hatch, and the script stopped with
Jebediah aboard the pod on the pad. The next launch, 26 s later, called `launch_vessel`
with `recover=True`, and the game seated Bill. The closing save lists Jebediah as
Missing, and the save's setting `MissingCrewsRespawn = False` means he will not return
by himself. The career save was not loaded or changed.

## Cause and contributing factors

The script stopped on an error with a kerbal still aboard, and nothing checked for crew
before the next launch. `launch_vessel(recover=True)` removed the occupied pod in a way
that marked its crew Missing rather than returning them to the roster *(strong
inference, the mechanism inside kRPC was not examined)*. The hatch was covered because
the generator placed a motor at 0 degrees, where revision A5B and the abort pods had
left it clear.

## Corrective action and acceptance

Every launch now first recovers, explicitly, any crewed vessel standing at a launch
site, and refuses to launch if one cannot be recovered. Motors are placed clear of the
hatch. For the campaign this is a requirement on any automated launch sequence. A
script never leaves a kerbal in a vessel it is about to replace, and it never relies on
a launch call to clear crew. Whether to restore Jebediah by editing the verification
save's roster is the operator's decision.

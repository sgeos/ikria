# VR-0009, starting-parts hop development

> **Navigation**: [Verification](README.md) | [Vehicle](../vehicles/IKR-TS-001.md)

**Status.** Two uncrewed mechanics flights completed on 2026-11-17, reported by Codex.
No career mission is cleared. The operator authorized rehearsals and debug features
in "Ikria Physical Verification", with the career still at its starting state. The
operator also authorized provisional vehicle and mission record sections.

## Purpose and configuration

Test a recoverable first-hop article with parts already purchased in the career, and
identify obstacles to rehearsing E0 without risking crew. Both flights used the
verification career, identified through the existing save guard before operations.
A checkpoint, `ipv-before-early-rehearsal`, preserves the earlier monolith setup.

The read-only career inventory found 10,000 funds, zero science, only the start node,
and initial facility levels. All eight article parts were purchased. The career file
hash was unchanged between the inventory and the final check. No research, purchase,
launch, save or debug command was sent to that career.

The `DIFFICULTY`, `CAREER`, `CommNetParams` and `AdvancedParams` groups matched between
the career file and the verification checkpoint. Acceleration limits for parts and
crew and part pressure limits were disabled in both. Reentry heating was 1, CommNet
was enabled, and the range modifier was 0.65. Reload permissions differ intentionally.
The verification save already had about two million funds and additional research,
so its accumulated progress and payouts do not represent a fresh career.

## Procedure and deviations

1. Derive revision A from the operator's local `IkriaT0` craft, reducing the Flea to
   70 fuel units and 35 percent thrust. Preserve the eight-part arrangement.
2. Attempt empty-capsule placement. Calls from Flight and Space Center each timed out
   after 60 seconds. Separate reads showed no test article had appeared. The scene
   setter successfully returned the game to the Space Center between attempts.
3. Place the article with Jebediah aboard and the engine off. This succeeded. Send him
   outside, release the ladder, recover him, and return to the empty capsule. Verify
   zero crew aboard and Jebediah available in the roster before ignition.
4. Ignite through `Engine.active`, without active attitude control. Sample telemetry
   with a 0.25-second wall-time delay between batches. Deploy through
   `Parachute.deploy` only on descent after burnout, below 1,500 m above terrain and
   when the game reports safe deployment. Save the landed result and recover it.
5. Repeat with revision B, using 35 fuel units and 20 percent thrust, after removing
   and recovering Jebediah again.

**Control exception.** Both empty capsules reported `ControlState.none` and no link.
Part-level ignition and parachute deployment nevertheless worked. These commands were
used as verification instrumentation under the operator's authorization. They do not
establish career-valid control under R2 or R3. No Keleusma flight program was used.

**Debug accounting.** No debug-service setter was called. After both flights, infinite
propellant, infinite electricity, crash immunity, unbreakable joints and temperature
immunity all read false. They were not sampled before the flights, so the record does
not prove their state throughout. The final probe now records these flags before
ignition. The earlier monolith funds and technology changes were left in place.

**Launch-hang hypothesis.** The absence of a control source is a plausible cause.
kRPC's [published 0.7.0 launch implementation](https://github.com/krpc/krpc/blob/v0.7.0/service/SpaceCenter/src/Services/SpaceCenter.cs)
includes that preflight check. Crewed placement succeeded with the same article. No
specific warning was captured, so the cause remains unconfirmed. The dialog the
operator subsequently identified was Jebediah's recovery summary, not evidence of a
launch warning. The test did not require a manual save load.

## Limits and next work

Each revision flew once. Sampling was asynchronous and depended on wall-clock
scheduling, so neither repeatability nor timing determinism was established. The
recorded flight angles use the supplied body reference frame and are not a validated
surface-attitude controller. The change in mass after crew removal requires accounting
before treating these flights as a crewed performance model.

No phase-specific abort was tested. No science-collection profile or contract was
cleared. Recoveries changed the verification save's progress, but transaction timing
and a fresh-career payout were not established. No career funds projection is inferred
from them.

T12 remains open. The next work is a live Keleusma flight path, phase-specific aborts,
repeatable nominal flights, and a documented qualification procedure for an uncrewed
starting-parts article that lacks a probe core. The verification-only part controls
must not silently become the career control policy.

# M-0001, Verification Hop and Abort Development

> **Navigation**: [Missions](README.md) | [Vehicle](../vehicles/IKR-TS-001.md)

**Date and author.** 2026-11-17, Codex.
**Status.** Development report in provisional sections, not career clearance.

## Operational plan

This report reconstructs the operational sequence recorded during the session. It is
not a retrospective claim of a formal flight-readiness approval. The objective was
to establish the bridge, Keleusma execution and command lifecycle, with acknowledged
ignition and deployment, intact recovery and replayable evidence.

The article was IKR-TS-001B, eight starting parts, 35 solid-fuel units and a 20 percent
thrust limit. Crew were used only for pad placement and recovered before ignition.
The sequence was telemetry-only monitoring, pad abort, developmental hops, and an
external coast abort. No career contracts or completion criteria were served.

The flight was a near-vertical ballistic hop from the Space Center with passive fins
and parachute recovery. No launch window optimization, orbital transfer or landing
footprint targeting was attempted. Propellant was fixed by the tested craft. A priced
budget, delta-v margin, electrical endurance model and financial ledger remain absent.
No extravehicular propellant was part of the flight profile.

The bridge's no-go criteria were wrong save, wrong vessel, crew aboard, changed parts,
wrong fuel or thrust setting, an active motor, a deployed chute, warp or disabled
physics. Abort inhibited pad ignition and otherwise waited for burnout and safe descent.
A burning solid motor could not be shut down. Risks included incorrect telemetry
frames, incomplete abort envelopes, communication failure and touchdown classification.

## Results and timeline

[VR-0010](../verification/VR-0010.md) and its raw journals supply the timeline and
configuration hashes. The final normal flight reached 1,239.04 metres above terrain,
completed in 136.46 game seconds and produced 6,824 acknowledged commands. The
coast-abort flight reached 1,239.19 metres, completed in 135.94 seconds and produced
6,798 acknowledged commands. Both were recovered with all eight parts. Their replayed
commands match exactly. The coast abort latched at sequence 669 after burnout.

[I-0001](../incidents/I-0001.md) records the earlier article loss caused by incorrect
velocity framing. [I-0002](../incidents/I-0002.md) records the intact landing whose
bounce stopped the software before completion. Both corrections were tested. These
runs used changing software and do not establish statistical repeatability.

## Costs, science and lessons

No campaign cost or science claim is made. Verification-save funds and recovery science
are recorded as observations, not attributed payouts. The career file remained
unchanged. The two incident reports and anomaly log retain the lessons about moving
frames and ground-contact classification. Qualification of the empty starting capsule's
control authority remains unresolved.

## Limits of comparison

This is evidence about software operating in the game's physics. Exact telemetry,
instantaneous communication, simplified atmosphere and parachute behaviour, and the
absence of sensor uncertainty limit what it demonstrates. It is not evidence of
real-world vehicle buildability, flight qualification or crew safety. Active attitude
actuation was not exercised because the empty capsule had no control source.
